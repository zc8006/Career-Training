## Session

- **Topic:** Feeling down at work because a colleague joined a project but the user did not/could not.

## Actual Output

At first the user said:

> 今日は気分が、んーん、昨日も悪いですね、今日は、んー、原因はあの、んー、新しい、んー、私の同僚はあの、プロジェクトに参加、入りましたね、んー、けど、ま、はい、まあ、けど、んー、私も、はい、入れ、私が、んー、入れませんでしたね。そして気分が、んー、悪いですね

The meaning conveyed: the user still feels bad; a colleague joined a project and the user could not join; the situation affected their mood.

**Do not presume what 新しい modifies.** It may refer to a new colleague or project; the user did not clarify.

A compact natural version fitting the meaning expressed:

> **昨日も今日も、あまり気分がよくないです。**  
> **同僚がプロジェクトに入りましたが、私は入れませんでした。**  
> **それで、少し落ち込んでいます。**

The user later produced, incrementally:

> そのプロジェクトに入りませんでした

and finally:

> でも、そのプロジェクト、に入りませんでした

The trainer repeatedly pushed a specific phrasing instead of returning to a conversation.

## Grammar / Vocabulary

### 入りませんでした vs. 入れませんでした

- **入りませんでした** = did not enter / did not join (plain past negative of 入る). This says what happened, without specifically conveying inability.
- **入れませんでした** = could not enter / was unable to join (past negative potential of 入る).
- For the expressed meaning *wanted/could have hoped to join but could not*, **プロジェクトに入れませんでした** is more precise.
- The user's **入りませんでした is grammatical**. It was a serious trainer error to call it 「完璧」 as if it were the same form. Next time explain the difference briefly, without making the user endlessly repeat it.

### 感情

User:
> 今日は気分が、昨日も悪いですね

Natural for "yesterday and today":
> **昨日も今日も、あまり気分がよくないです。**

If psychological disappointment:
> **少し落ち込んでいます。**

気分が悪い can also indicate physical discomfort. Preserve the user's intended emotion and, if necessary, ask.

### Understanding support

User:
> 全部我一度お願いします

Likely asking to hear the whole opening again; the trainer shortened and reworded it, rather than repeating the original as required by Listening Rescue.

User:
> 何を質問か?もう一度お願いします、同僚、こう、わからないですね

User:
> 私は、えーんー、あなたの意味が、私は、んー、もう一度、あなた話すこと

User:
> 練習いたしますか

User:
> 単語が何ですか、さかね、なんか

The user repeatedly indicated confusion about the *question/meaning/sound*. The exact sound 「さか」 cannot reliably be identified from the conversation, and the trainer speculated it might mean 「その」. Do not treat that as a verified recognition.

Useful clarification phrases to teach **in context only**:
- **どういう意味ですか？** = What does that mean?
- **もう一度、同じ文を言ってください。** = Please say that same sentence again.
- **「同僚」はどういう意味ですか？** = What does dōryō mean?

Vocabulary:
- **同僚（どうりょう）** = colleague / 同事
- **プロジェクトに入る** = join a project
- **落ち込む（おちこむ）** = feel down

## Listening Growth

- Repeated requests for restatement show self-advocacy, but no valid numeric first-pass comprehension rate can be computed.
- The trainer often *explained or changed* the original question instead of repeating it verbatim at normal speed, followed by a slower repetition.
- The vocabulary question around 「同僚」 and an unclear sound was not resolved carefully.
- Once misunderstanding appeared, the conversation was not returned to natural flow.

**Required protocol**:
1. Repeat the same utterance at normal speed.
2. Repeat the same utterance more slowly.
3. Identify only the problematic sound/word.
4. Explain briefly, using Chinese only if still needed.
5. Return to the same complete utterance and conversation.

## Progress vs Day 25

### Keep
- Real-life emotions and their causes as genuine conversation topics.
- The user's spontaneous contrast: 同僚が入った / 自分は入れなかった.
- Ability to ask for repetitions and pause when meaning is unclear.

### Upgrade
- From fragments to 2–3 consecutive natural sentences about the situation and its emotional impact.
- Discriminate past negative vs past negative potential in a meaningful communicative context: 入りませんでした vs 入れませんでした.
- Connect ideas with が / けど / それで.

### Retire
- No new expression reliably demonstrated as fluent/active; do not prematurely retire old vocabulary.

### Repair (trainer)
1. Free Talk must not be converted into mechanical repeat-after-me drills.
2. Stop saying 「完璧」 for grammatically valid but semantically different wording.
3. Follow Listening Rescue verbatim; do not reword first.
4. Don't guess unclear heard sounds or meanings; confirm the word.
5. Let the user finish a thought before correcting at most 1–2 items.
6. Avoid prematurely ending a 15-minute session. If the user chooses to end, respect that.
7. Respond empathically and naturally to the user's real-life concern, without treating it only as grammar practice.

## Day 27 Focus

- **Target duration**: ~15 min, at least 80% natural Japanese.
- Begin from a genuine conversation, not a memorized sentence.
- Naturally revisit the work/mood theme if the user wants to; otherwise transition to an everyday topic.
- Challenge the user to describe **what happened → why they felt that way → what they think/do next** in 2–3 short sentences, without demanding exact repetition.
- Reuse **同僚**, **プロジェクトに入る**, **気分転換**, **習慣**, **つい** only when natural.
- If the user says 「もう一度お願いします」, repeat the **exact** previous Japanese sentence before any explanation.
- Track improvements based on actual observed performance, not guessed metrics.
